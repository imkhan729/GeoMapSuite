Direct Answer & Core Functionality

The Tropic of Cancer is the northern latitude limit where the Sun can appear directly overhead at solar noon. It is commonly mapped near 23.5° north; the Sun reaches its northernmost subsolar latitude around the June solstice.

Tropic of Cancer reference and map accuracy

An approximate reference for the northern tropic

This map uses 23.4364° N as a fixed cartographic reference for the Tropic of Cancer. The astronomical tropic follows changes in Earth’s axial tilt, so this is not a date-specific solar calculation. Its WGS 84 parallel length is a model-derived reference, not a ground-route measurement. The displayed line and basemap are for geographic reference, not surveying or legal boundary work.

Practical Takeaway: This is a mathematical length along a constant-latitude parallel on the reference ellipsoid, not the distance of a ground route or a measurement of terrain.

Understanding Your Results & Practical Interpretation

Latitude is an approximate reference

The interactive line uses 23.4364° N, a conventional mapping value. Educational sources often round the Tropic of Cancer to 23.5° N or 23°27′ N. Those rounded labels are suitable for general geography, while precise astronomical work requires a date and an ephemeris.

The solstice explains the line’s meaning

Near the June solstice, the Sun’s subsolar point reaches its northern annual extreme. The Tropic of Cancer marks the approximate northern limit where the Sun can be directly overhead at solar noon.

Published crossing counts use different conventions

One commonly published list names these 17 countries or territories: Algeria, Niger, Libya, Egypt, Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, Oman, India, Bangladesh, Myanmar, China, Taiwan, Mexico, the Bahamas, Western Sahara, Mauritania, and Mali. Western Sahara is disputed, and Taiwan’s political status is disputed. Some maps also discuss a near-touch or boundary contact with Chad, so a total depends on the chosen boundary dataset and counting convention.

Mathematical Methodology & Geodetic Accuracy

Approximate Tropic Latitude and WGS 84 Parallel Length

The map uses 23.4364° N as a static, commonly used cartographic reference. The astronomical tropic is associated with Earth’s obliquity and is not a permanently fixed latitude. The displayed parallel length is calculated on the WGS 84 ellipsoid.

φref = 23.4364° N; N = a / √(1 − e² sin²φ); C = 2πN cosφ

Troubleshooting & Geographic Edge Cases

Why do maps show 23.5°, 23°27′, or 23.4364° N?

They use different rounded or conventional reference values. The actual astronomical tropic is related to Earth’s changing axial tilt, while this interactive map uses one documented fixed approximation.

Why does the map not show the exact Sun position for today?

This is a static geography reference map. A date-specific subsolar point requires astronomical calculations and changes throughout the year.

Why do websites list different numbers of countries?

Some lists include or exclude disputed territories, Taiwan, boundary contacts, and islands. Always check what the author counts as a country crossing.
