Distance Between ZIP Codes

Distance between two US ZIP codes with population data and interactive map visualization.

Direct Answer & Core Functionality

The Distance Between ZIP Codes calculator computes the high-precision straight-line geodesic distance between the geographic centroids of any two 5-digit United States ZIP Codes using US Census Bureau ZIP Code Tabulation Area (ZCTA) coordinate data and WGS84 ellipsoidal mathematics. It outputs distance in miles and kilometers, compass bearings, the midpoint location, and provides full GeoJSON, KML, and CSV export.

A national shipping logistics coordinator calculates the baseline straight-line distance between ZIP Code 90210 (Beverly Hills, CA; 34.0901° N, 118.4065° W) and ZIP Code 10001 (New York, NY; 40.7506° N, 73.9972° W).

Step-by-Step Mathematical Process

  1. Resolve Census ZCTA 90210 centroid: Latitude 34.0901° N, Longitude 118.4065° W.
  2. Resolve Census ZCTA 10001 centroid: Latitude 40.7506° N, Longitude 73.9972° W.
  3. Execute Karney inverse geodesic algorithm on WGS84 ellipsoid: s12 = 3,944,957 meters.
  4. Convert to statute miles: 3,944,957 m / 1,609.344 m/mi = 2,451.28 miles (3,944.96 km).
  5. Calculate initial forward azimuth: 72.4° (East-North-East departure heading).
  6. Determine midpoint coordinate: 39.4674° N, 97.4382° W (near Cloud County, Kansas).
Practical Takeaway: Because the Earth is an oblate spheroid, the shortest path arches northward across Colorado, Nebraska, and Iowa rather than following a straight line on a flat Mercator projection.

Understanding Your Results & Practical Interpretation

USPS Delivery ZIP Codes vs. US Census Bureau ZCTAs

ZIP Codes (Zone Improvement Plan) were created by the United States Postal Service in 1963 as mail distribution postal routes, not geographic polygons. For cartographic and spatial analysis, the US Census Bureau defines ZIP Code Tabulation Areas (ZCTAs), which represent generalized areal approximations of postal delivery routes. Our tool calculates distances between official ZCTA internal point centroids.

Straight-Line (Geodesic) vs. Highway Driving Mileage

This tool computes straight-line "as the crow flies" geodesic distance on the WGS84 reference ellipsoid. Paved road driving distance will almost always be 15% to 35% longer (higher in mountainous or coastal terrain) due to highway curves, grade topography, and street grid networks.

Initial vs. Final Compass Bearings

Because lines of longitude converge toward the North and South Poles, following the shortest geodesic Great-Circle arc requires continuous adjustments in compass heading. The initial bearing indicates the direction to point your compass at the start; the final bearing indicates your heading as you arrive.

Troubleshooting & Geographic Edge Cases

Why does my ZIP code return an "Unassigned or Missing ZCTA" message?

Some valid USPS ZIP codes are assigned exclusively to Post Office box clusters, large volume commercial mail receivers (e.g. government agencies), or military APO/FPO bases. Because these facilities lack a distinct residential land distribution area, the US Census Bureau does not generate a ZCTA polygon for them.

Why is the calculated mileage shorter than Google Maps driving directions?

This tool measures the direct straight-line distance ("as the crow flies") through coordinate space along the curve of the Earth. Real-world driving requires following paved roadways, highways, traffic patterns, and detours around rivers or mountain passes, typically adding 15% to 35% more distance.

How accurate are the centroid coordinates for rural ZIP codes?

ZCTA centroids represent the official internal point computed by the US Census Bureau. For dense urban ZIP codes (e.g., Manhattan), the centroid is within 0.5 miles of any address. For expansive rural ZIP codes spanning hundreds of square miles, the centroid represents the geometric center, which may be located far from town centers.
